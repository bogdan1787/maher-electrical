Honest Answers About Cost

Every electrical job is different. The age of the wiring, the size of the property, how accessible the work is and what needs certifying all shape the quote — which is why a genuine contractor cannot price work properly over the phone without knowing the details.

That is also why this guide deliberately contains no price list. Instead, it explains what drives the cost of electrical work and how to compare quotes like-for-like. What Affects Your Quote

Six things that shape almost every electrical quote.

📋

Scope of the Work

A single socket swap and a full rewire are very different jobs. The clearer the scope, the more accurate the quote can be.

🔧

Materials & Components

Quality cable, fittings and protective devices vary in specification — what is included matters as much as the work itself.

🏚

Age & Condition of Wiring

Older installations can hide surprises: perished cable, no earth continuity, outdated boards. A survey reduces the unknown.

🏠

Access & Property Type

Period homes, attic runs, solid walls and commercial units all take different amounts of time and equipment to work in.

⏰

Urgency & Call-Out Timing

Evening, weekend and emergency call-outs are attended outside normal hours, which affects how the visit is arranged.

📜

Testing & Certification

Testing, certifying and documenting the work is part of a proper job — and it protects you when selling, renting or claiming on insurance.

How to Compare Quotes

A lower figure is only good value if it covers the same work. Use these checks before you decide who to hire.

  • Compare like-for-like scope — make sure each quote covers the same work, not just a similar-sounding job.
  • Confirm the contractor is registered and insured — ask for registration details and proof of insurance before work starts.
  • Check what is included — materials, testing, certification and VAT should all be clear in writing.
  • Ask what could change the price — a good contractor will explain the unknowns and how variations are handled.
  • Be wary of vague quotes — one line for a big job usually means the detail has not been thought through.
  • Cheapest is not always safest — a quote that skips certification or protection is not really cheaper in the long run.

Questions to Ask Before Hiring

Before you book any electrician — us included — these questions will tell you a lot about how the job will go.

  • Are you a registered electrical contractor, and with which body?
  • Are you insured, and can I see proof?
  • Will I get a certificate for the work?
  • What is the timeline, and who will be on site?
  • What exactly is included in the quote?
  • Who handles grant paperwork for solar or EV work?

Why do electricians' quotes vary so much?

Because jobs that sound the same can differ hugely once you look at the wiring, access, materials and certification involved. Some quotes are also less complete than others. The fairest comparison is on scope, not headline — ask each contractor to set out exactly what is included.

Is it cheaper to do electrical work myself?

Electrical work carries real risks, and mistakes are not always obvious until something fails. Certified work also matters when you sell or rent a property. We would always recommend a registered, insured contractor — and if budget is a concern, we can discuss phasing the work and prioritising what is essential first.

Does home insurance cover electrical repairs?

It depends on your policy and the cause of the fault. Some policies cover damage caused by specific events, while others do not. We provide certification and a clear record of the work, which can help if you need to make a claim, and we recommend checking directly with your insurer.

How do call-out charges work?

Call-out charges depend on the time and duration of the visit and the distance to your property. We confirm what applies before we attend, so there are no surprises. Call or WhatsApp 085 200 8988 and we will explain exactly what is involved.

How do I get the most accurate quote?

Give us as much detail as you can and, where possible, arrange a site visit. A survey lets us check the existing installation, access and materials, so the quote reflects the real work. If anything changes once we start, we agree it with you before proceeding.
